# Universidad Europea de Canarias (UEC) — Grado en Medicina

## Overview

Universidad Europea de Canarias (UEC) is a private university in La Orotava, Tenerife, part of the broader Universidad Europea group (owned by Permira). Its Medicine degree is the only private Medicine program in the Canary Islands, making it the sole private option for students based in the archipelago and a unique geographic proposition for students seeking an island setting.

The program launches its inaugural cohort in September 2026 with 50 seats. The six-year, 360-ECTS degree is taught primarily in Spanish with selected subjects in English (Years 2–4). Clinical training begins in Year 3: first through primary care (Atención Primaria via Servicio Canario de Salud) in the Medicina familiar module (3 ECTS PAE), and in university hospital settings, with full clinical rotations across all specialties in Year 6 within Grupo Hospiten's three university hospitals in Tenerife.

The admissions model is notably transparent and quantifiable: 90% of the score derives from the average of the student's 1st-year Bachillerato grades (Ciencias de la Salud branch only), and 10% from an in-house English language test. A mandatory Spanish B2 test (eliminatory, non-scoring) is required for non-native speakers. ## Program Options

UEC offers a single Medicine degree track: 6-year presencial program, primarily in Spanish, with four subjects taught in English (Comunicación y divulgación científica in Y2, Medicina basada en la evidencia y la tecnología in Y2, Gestión sanitaria sostenible in Y3, and Medicina preventiva y salud pública in Y4). There is no bilingual track, no online variant, and no separate campus or degree pathway.

The program is housed in the Facultad de Ciencias de la Salud in La Orotava, Tenerife. The campus includes a dedicated hospital simulado — a multi-space simulation facility with advanced equipment aligned to each Medicine subject — used for pre-clinical skills development before students begin hospital rotations in Year 3.

## Program Structure

The curriculum spans six years of 60 ECTS each (360 ECTS total). The credit distribution is: Formación básica 90 ECTS, Obligatorias 201 ECTS, Optativas 9 ECTS, Prácticas Asistenciales Externas (PAE) 54 ECTS, TFG 6 ECTS.

The curriculum uses an integrated systems-based approach rather than a discipline-by-discipline structure. Major organ systems are covered holistically across years rather than as separate anatomy/physiology/pathology courses:

**Years 1–2 — Basic biomedical sciences:** Systems anatomy and physiology (cardiovascular, locomotor, nervous system, respiratory, gastrointestinal, endocrine/reproductive), biochemistry, cell biology, genetics, biostatistics/epidemiology, and foundations of clinical methodology. Year 2 includes an "Introducción preclínica" module (6 ECTS) as a bridge toward clinical competencies.

**Year 3 — Clinical integration:** Pathophysiology and semiología (12 ECTS integrated block), pharmacology fundamentals, surgery basics, Anatomía Patológica, and the first PAE block (Medicina familiar, 3 ECTS in Atención Primaria). Three English-language subjects across Years 2–4 develop academic communication skills and evidence-based medicine.

**Years 4–5 — Clinical specialties:** Systematic coverage of all major clinical specialties: cardiovascular, digestive, respiratory, endocrinology, neurology, infectious diseases, hematology, pediatrics, psychiatry, gynecology/obstetrics, dermatology, ophthalmology, geriatrics, oncology, emergency medicine, and more.

**Year 6 — Clinical rotations (PAE, 51 ECTS) + TFG (6 ECTS) + Genómica clínica (3 ECTS):** Near-full-year hospital immersion across 10 defined rotations, covering medicina interna, urgencias, psychiatry, general surgery, primary care, pediatrics, two blocks of medical specialties, surgical specialties, and gynecology/obstetrics.

Electives (3 ECTS per year): In Years 2, 3, and 5, students choose one elective from two options per year. Available electives include IA en salud (Y2), ECG interpretation (Y3), ultrasound (Y5), and telemedicine/digital health (Y5).

A distinctive research thread runs across Years 1–5: biostatistics (Y1), evidence-based medicine (Y2), precision medicine (Y3), and "Inquietud investigadora" (Y5, 6 ECTS research initiative). The final year curriculum includes a genomics module covering clinical application of basic sciences.

## Teaching Methodology

UEC's pedagogical model for Medicine is described as experiential, competency-based, and integrating high-fidelity simulation with real clinical environments. The hospital simulado provides dedicated simulation spaces equipped for each academic subject, with rotations organized in sub-groups to maximize instructional quality. This allows progressive skill development before students enter live patient environments. The university publishes a student-to-teacher ratio of 1:1–2 for practical simulation sessions.

The curriculum integrates evidence-based practice throughout: the Year 2 subject "Medicina basada en la evidencia y la tecnología" (6 ECTS, English) explicitly trains students in applying research evidence to clinical decision-making, a skill with direct MIR relevance. "Herramientas de investigación: bioestadística y epidemiología" in Year 1 grounds students in quantitative methodology from the start.

The English-language subjects embedded across Years 2–4 are not language-learning courses but are subject-matter taught in English — developing academic English in a medical context without requiring a bilingual track. This is a strategic differentiator for internationally-oriented students.

The Year 6 clinical rotation block (51 ECTS PAE) covers 10 structured rotation areas with explicit specialty definitions, providing systematic breadth across all primary MIR specialties.

Campus simulation facilities include: Laboratorio de Ciencias Básicas, Laboratorio de Estructura y Función (histology, integrated anatomy/physiology), Hospital Simulado (multi-space facility with advanced equipment per subject), Sala de Simulación Compleja (high-fidelity simulators and actors for complex scenarios), and Sala Task Training (low/medium-fidelity simulation for technical skills).

## Admission Model

The UEC Canarias Medicine admissions process uses an entirely quantifiable, science-exam-free model. No internal Biology/Chemistry/Physics exam is required. No EBAU/PAU score is used for ranking (it is required only as a legal access credential before enrolment). No interview is required.

**Admission score formula:**
| Component | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Nota media — 1st year Bachillerato (all subjects except Religion) | 90% | Must be Ciencias de la Salud branch. Not 2nd year; not full GPA. |
| In-house English test | 10% | Reading, grammar, vocabulary, listening. Minimum B1 required to pass. |
| **Total** | **100%** | |

**Eliminatory filters (mandatory, non-scoring):**
- **Spanish B2 test:** For non-native Spanish speakers. Failure = automatic disqualification regardless of other scores.
- **Prueba Talent UE:** Competency and study-behaviour questionnaire (~30 min, 139 items). Mandatory for all candidates but does NOT affect the admission score. Failure to complete = exclusion from the process.
- **Bachillerato branch restriction:** Only Ciencias de la Salud bachillerato (or health-area Ciclo Formativo Superior) is accepted. Candidates from other bachillerato branches are automatically excluded.

**Legal access requirement (separate from selection):** If admitted, students must provide proof of legal university access (PAU/EBAU card, Técnico Superior title, UNED credential, or homologation of foreign Bachillerato) before the enrolment deadline. Admission is conditional until this is received.

**Credit recognition restriction:** Students accessing via any pre-university route (national EvAU or international equivalent) must sign a formal waiver renouncing all credit recognition (convalidaciones) for any previously studied university-level subjects. No exceptions.

**Virtual process:** Both the English test and the Prueba Talent UE are conducted online. Candidates connect on a confirmed date. No travel to Tenerife is required for the admissions stage.

## Admission Score Distribution

Detailed scoring breakdown is fully quantifiable. The formula is:

**Final score = 0.90 × (nota media 1st year Bachillerato) + 0.10 × (English test score)**

Because 90% of the score is the pre-existing 1st-year Bachillerato average, students know their primary score component before applying — there is no uncertainty from an unpredictable science exam. The 10% English test contribution means that a student with a 8.0 Bachillerato average can gain at most 1 point from a perfect English test, which is unlikely to be decisive at this stage.

**Implications:**
- A student with nota media 8.0 + perfect English (10): 0.90×8.0 + 0.10×10 = 7.2 + 1.0 = 8.2 final score
- A student with nota media 7.0 + perfect English: 6.3 + 1.0 = 7.3 final score
- A student with nota media 9.5 + B1 English (minimum pass): approximately 8.55 + small English contribution

No published cutoff exists for the inaugural cohort. Given 50 seats and the enrollment stage, selectivity is expected to be low to moderate. Students with a 1st-year Bachillerato average above 7.5 in Ciencias de la Salud and functional English should be competitive.

The B2 Spanish requirement (for non-native speakers) is a hard gate: candidates who fail it are eliminated regardless of all other scores. Non-native speakers must treat Spanish preparation as a prerequisite, not optional.

## Accommodation and Cost of Living

La Orotava is a historic municipality in the interior of Tenerife, situated on the slopes of Teide in the Orotava Valley. The town itself is approximately 15–20 km from Santa Cruz de Tenerife (the capital) and has no direct metro connection. Local transport options are mainly bus (guagua) and private vehicle.

The Canary Islands generally offer a lower cost of living than mainland Spain, particularly in non-tourist interior municipalities. Rough estimate for a student living independently in La Orotava: 700–950 EUR/month excluding tuition (accommodation, food, transport, personal), significantly lower than Madrid or Barcelona. Students arriving from mainland Spain or abroad may face higher initial setup costs.

Island-specific considerations: flights between Tenerife and the Spanish mainland are frequent but add up for students who travel home regularly; car ownership or rental is practical for independence. Social and extracurricular options are more limited than in major mainland cities.

Yes, for non-native Spanish speakers. A Spanish language test is integrated into the admissions process. Failure to reach B2 results in automatic disqualification, regardless of all other scores.

**What are the costs for 2026-27?**

Annual teaching fee: 18,900 EUR (payable in 10 or 12 monthly instalments). Approximate Year 1 total: ~22,560 EUR. ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED.

**Can I pay tuition in instalments?**
Yes. The annual teaching fee (18,900 EUR) can be fractioned over 10 or 12 monthly payments. Contact your enrollment advisor to confirm the terms.

**When do clinical rotations begin?**
Clinical contacts begin in Year 3 (Medicina familiar, 3 ECTS PAE in Atención Primaria, and some hospital exposure). Full hospital rotations take place in Year 6 across Grupo Hospiten's three university hospitals in Tenerife.

**Which hospitals will I train in?**
Hospital Universitario Hospiten Bellevue, Hospital Universitario Hospiten Rambla, and Hospital Universitario Hospiten Sur (all in Tenerife, part of Grupo Hospiten). Atención Primaria rotations are in Servicio Canario de Salud centres. ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED.

**Are any subjects taught in English?**
Yes. Four subjects are taught in English: Comunicación y divulgación científica (Y2), Medicina basada en la evidencia y la tecnología (Y2), Gestión sanitaria sostenible (Y3), and Medicina preventiva y salud pública (Y4). All other subjects are in Spanish.

**Is the degree WFME accredited?**
WFME accreditation for UEC Canarias specifically is PENDING_CONFIRMATION. Students planning to practice outside Spain or in countries with WFME requirements must verify this before enrolling.

**Can I get credit recognition for subjects I studied at another university?**
Not if you are accessing the degree via a pre-university route (EvAU or international equivalent). Credit recognition (convalidaciones) is explicitly excluded for pre-university access applicants — you must sign a formal waiver as part of enrolment.

**I am on the waiting list. What are my chances?**
Unknown. The waiting list is managed in a strict order determined by the admissions committee. If admitted candidates do not pay the reservation fee within 5 business days, their seat moves to the next person on the list. Monitor the pre-student portal actively.
