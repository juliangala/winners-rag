# Universidad de Córdoba (UCO) — Grado en Medicina

## Overview

The Universidad de Córdoba (UCO), founded in 1972, is a public research university in Andalucía offering the Grado en Medicina through its Facultad de Medicina y Enfermería. As a member institution of the Andalucía Distrito Único Andaluz (DUA) admissions system, UCO's Medicine programme admits students exclusively on the basis of the nota de admisión — no proprietary entrance exam, interview, or language test is required. The programme is delivered across the Facultad de Medicina y Enfermería with clinical training centred on the Hospital Universitario Reina Sofía, one of the largest tertiary referral centres in southern Spain and a nationally recognised centre for transplantation and complex surgery. UCO participates in the European university alliance YUFE (Young Universities for the Future of Europe). Tuition is set under Junta de Andalucía pricing regulation, making UCO one of the most cost-effective Medicine programmes available in Spain.

## Program Options

UCO offers a single Grado en Medicina delivered entirely in Spanish. There are no parallel tracks in other languages, no online or hybrid delivery modes, and no binational double-degree programmes within this degree. The programme follows the national framework established by Orden ECI/332/2008.

## Program Structure

The programme is structured across six academic years totalling 360 ECTS, following the national regulatory framework:

| Year | Key Content Areas |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | Human Anatomy, Biochemistry and Molecular Biology, Biostatistics and Computing, Cell Biology and Genetics, General Physiology, Medical Terminology |
| Year 2 | Histology and Embryology, Medical Physiology, Microbiology and Parasitology, Immunology, Pharmacology I, Introduction to Clinical Medicine |
| Year 3 | Pathological Anatomy, Internal Medicine I, General Surgery, Pharmacology II, Psychiatry, Radiology and Physical Medicine |
| Year 4 | Internal Medicine II, Paediatrics, Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Ophthalmology, ENT, Dermatology and Venereology |
| Year 5 | Traumatology, Urology, Neurology, Preventive Medicine and Public Health, Legal and Forensic Medicine, Primary Care |
| Year 6 | Clinical Rotations: Internal Medicine, Surgery, Obstetrics and Gynaecology, Paediatrics, Psychiatry, Emergency Medicine, Primary Care + Final Year Project (TFG, 6 ECTS) |

## Teaching Methodology

The UCO Medicine programme integrates traditional lecture-based instruction in early years with increasing use of problem-based learning (PBL), clinical simulation, and skills laboratories. Anatomy dissection using cadaveric material is incorporated from Year 1. From Year 3, students begin supervised integration into hospital departments. Clinical simulation centres equipped with mannequins and standardised patient scenarios support procedural skills development before patients are encountered in supervised clinical environments. The Facultad de Medicina y Enfermería maintains research-linked teaching in areas such as oncology, metabolic disease, and neuroscience.

## Clinical Training

Clinical training at UCO is delivered through the Servicio Andaluz de Salud (SAS) network under formal agreement with the Junta de Andalucía:

- **Hospital Universitario Reina Sofía** — major tertiary referral and teaching hospital in Córdoba; one of Spain's largest hospitals by bed count and nationally recognised for liver transplantation, cardiac surgery, and oncology
- **Complejo Hospitalario de Jaén** — supplementary clinical placements for certain rotations (subject to annual confirmation)
- **Centros de Salud (Primary Care)** — Andalusian Health Service primary care centres in the Córdoba health area; Year 6 Primary Care rotation

The Hospital Universitario Reina Sofía provides students with exposure to a high-complexity, high-volume clinical environment with specialist departments across all major medical and surgical disciplines. All rotations are supervised and assessed by hospital consultants and faculty staff.

## International Recognition and Opportunities

The UCO Grado en Medicina is fully recognised across the EU under the Bologna Process and EU Directive 2005/36/EC on professional qualifications recognition. Graduates may apply for medical registration in any EU member state following national procedures. Extra-EU recognition is subject to bilateral agreements or local homologation.

UCO is a member of the **YUFE (Young Universities for the Future of Europe)** alliance — a consortium of young, innovative European universities focused on research, knowledge transfer, and internationalisation. Mobility opportunities include:

- **Erasmus+** — clinical and academic placements at European partner institutions
- **YUFE Alliance** — collaborative programs with universities in Belgium, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Germany, Italy, Netherlands, and Romania
- **SICUE** — national inter-university exchange programme within Spain
- **IFMSA** — student-led international medical exchange network

## Admission Model

Access to Grado en Medicina at UCO is managed by the **Distrito Único Andaluz (DUA)**, which coordinates admissions to all public Andalusian universities through a single centralised application portal.

**Nota de admisión formula:**

```
Nota de admisión = 0.6 × Nota media Bachillerato
                 + 0.4 × EVAU fase general score
                 + best 2 specific-phase subjects × ponderation coefficient
```

- Base maximum (without bonus): **10.0000**
- Maximum with specific-phase bonus: **14.0000**
- No university-specific minimum grade beyond the national EVAU pass requirement (≥5.0)

**Ponderation coefficients for Medicina at UCO (Andalucía)** — set annually by the Comisión del Distrito Único Universitario de Andalucía and published in the BOJA:

| Subject | Coefficient |
|---|---|
| Biology | 0.2 # ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED |
| Chemistry | 0.2 # ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED |
| Physics | 0.2 # ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED |
| Mathematics II | 0.2 # ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED |

The best 2 eligible subjects are automatically selected to maximise the applicant's score. Students should confirm current ponderations at the official DUA portal before sitting specific-phase subjects.

**International students (PCE/UNED):** Students with international secondary qualifications must obtain a Credencial de Acceso through UNED via the PCE exams. The nota de admisión formula is identical. Students from EU systems may apply via the Credencial UE route. Selected-country homologation holders may apply directly.

## Admission Process

**Flow 1 — Spanish students (EVAU/PAU):**

1. Complete Bachillerato (2 years) and pass the EVAU fase general in Andalucía (inter-regional recognition applies for other regions).
2. Optionally sit specific-phase subjects to earn up to +4.0 bonus points on the nota de admisión.
3. Apply through the DUA portal (juntadeandalucia.es/sguit) during the application window after EVAU results are published.
4. List degree programmes and universities in order of preference (advisable to include multiple options as contingency).
5. Await adjudication results (initial list published mid-July; follow-up lists through October after waiting-list movements).
6. Accept the offer through the DUA portal and complete enrolment at UCO.
7. Complete online registration (automatrícula) by the deadline.

**Flow 2 — International students (PCE/UNED):**

1. Complete secondary education in the home country.
2. Obtain homologation of the secondary certificate OR sit PCE exams through UNED (available in Spain and at selected international centres).
3. Obtain the Credencial de Acceso from UNED.
4. Apply through the DUA admissions process for foreign students (separate phase with its own calendar, typically running February–June).
5. Provide certified and apostilled academic documents.
6. Await adjudication and complete enrolment at UCO.

## Risks and Considerations

1. **Nota de corte volatility:** The nota de corte fluctuates annually. A score above last year's cutoff does not guarantee admission; the DUA itself warns against treating historical scores as guaranteed thresholds.
2. **Regional quota mechanics:** The DUA system allocates seats across multiple Andalusian universities. Applicants should list multiple preferences as fallback options.
3. **Seat count variation:** Annual changes in offered seats directly affect the nota de corte. Monitor the DUA catalogue each year.
4. **PCE/UNED international pathway complexity:** International students must complete PCE exams in the right subjects and obtain their Credencial in time for the DUA foreign-student application window, which runs on an earlier calendar.
5. **Academic intensity:** Medicine at UCO is a demanding programme; the workload from Year 1 requires consistent engagement. Official permanence norms apply.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How is the nota de admisión calculated for UCO Medicine?**
A: Nota de admisión = 0.6 × Bachillerato average + 0.4 × EVAU fase general score + up to 2 specific-phase subjects × ponderation coefficient (currently 0.2 for Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Mathematics II at Andalusian universities including UCO). Maximum possible score: 14.0000. No additional university exam or interview applies.

**Q: What is the nota de corte and why does it change each year?**
A: The nota de corte is the score of the last student admitted in the ordinary round. It is not set in advance; it results from the interaction of available seats, applicant volume, and score distribution in that specific year. It should be treated as a reference point, not a guaranteed threshold.

**Q: Can international students (PCE/UNED) apply to UCO Medicine?**
A: Yes. International students must obtain a Credencial de Acceso through UNED — either via PCE exams (available at international centres) or EU credential recognition. They apply through the DUA foreign-student process. The nota de admisión formula and nota de corte are identical to domestic applicants.

**Q: What is the annual tuition fee for Medicine at UCO?**
A: Approximately 757.00 EUR per year for first-enrolment students (source: notasdecorte.es / Junta de Andalucía decree), subject to annual revision. This is among the lowest tuition rates in Spain for a Medicine programme.

**Q: When should I apply to UCO Medicine?**
A: The DUA domestic application window opens shortly after EVAU ordinaria results are published in late June. International students must apply through the DUA foreign-student process, which has an earlier calendar (typically opening in February). Always verify exact dates at juntadeandalucia.es/sguit.

**Q: What ponderation coefficients apply to Biology, Chemistry, and Physics for UCO Medicine?**
A: For Medicine at Andalusian universities including UCO, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Mathematics II have each carried a coefficient of 0.2 in recent years. This maximises the bonus from strong specific-phase Science results. Confirm current values in the BOJA before each EVAU sitting, as they are set annually.

**Q: What is the primary teaching hospital for UCO Medicine?**
A: The Hospital Universitario Reina Sofía in Córdoba — one of Spain's largest hospitals and a nationally recognised centre for liver transplantation, cardiac surgery, and oncology. Clinical rotations begin from Year 3.
