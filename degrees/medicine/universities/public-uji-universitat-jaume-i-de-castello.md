# Universitat Jaume I de Castelló (UJI) — Grado en Medicina

## Overview

The Universitat Jaume I de Castelló (UJI) is a public university founded in 1991, making it one of Spain's youngest universities. Its Grado en Medicina is among the most recent to be established at a Spanish public institution, with the degree launching approximately in 2021. The programme is housed within the Facultat de Ciències de la Salut on the main Campus del Riu Sec in Castellón de la Plana.

Admission to Medicine at UJI is governed by the Comunitat Valenciana's centralised admissions system (accesuniversitats.gva.es), managed by the Comissió Gestora de Processos Universitaris (CGPAU). There is no proprietary entrance exam, no interview, and no language test requirement beyond standard Spanish state access conditions. The admissions process is transparent and score-driven, based entirely on the nota de admisión derived from Bachillerato grades and EVAU performance.

UJI operates in a bilingual institutional environment (Spanish and Valencian), which is reflected in degree programme delivery. The main teaching hospital for clinical training is the Hospital Universitari General de Castelló. As a newer faculty, UJI's track record for MIR outcomes and clinical infrastructure is still being established, which is a relevant consideration for prospective students.

The nota de corte for Medicine at UJI in 2025-2026 was 13.085, placing it in the medium-high competitiveness band among Spanish public medical schools.

## Program Options

UJI offers a single Grado en Medicina programme, delivered in a bilingual Spanish/Valencian environment on the main campus in Castellón. There is no separate English-medium track, no distance-learning option, and no parallel international programme. All students follow a unified six-year curriculum regardless of their language background.

The programme is accessible via the accesuniversitats portal (Comunitat Valenciana) for Spanish EVAU candidates and via PCE/UNED or Credencial UE for international students. UJI's campus setting combines health sciences facilities with the broader technology-oriented infrastructure of the university.

## Program Structure

The Grado en Medicina at UJI follows the standard Spanish public medical degree structure across 6 years (360 ECTS):

| Year | Main focus |
|---|---|
| Year 1 | Foundational biomedical sciences: anatomy, physiology, biochemistry, cell biology |
| Year 2 | Pathophysiology, pharmacology, microbiology, genetics |
| Year 3 | Introduction to clinical medicine; first hospital placements begin |
| Year 4 | Clinical disciplines: internal medicine, surgery, paediatrics, obstetrics/gynaecology |
| Year 5 | Advanced clinical rotations across specialities |
| Year 6 | Rotatorio clínico (full-year supervised clinical placement across departments and primary care) + TFG |

Each academic year comprises approximately 60 ECTS. The final year is dedicated almost entirely to supervised clinical rotation across multiple hospital departments and primary care settings affiliated with UJI's Facultat de Ciències de la Salut.

## Teaching Methodology

UJI integrates a technology-oriented pedagogical approach consistent with the university's broader institutional identity. The Facultat de Ciències de la Salut applies a mixed model: theoretical instruction in lectures and seminars, laboratory-based practical work, clinical skills simulation, and progressively increasing direct patient contact from Year 3 onward.

Problem-based learning (PBL) and case-based discussions are incorporated alongside conventional lecture formats. The bilingual institutional context means students may encounter course materials and administrative communications in both Spanish and Valencian, though Medicine is accessible to non-Valencian speakers.

The teaching hospital affiliation — primarily Hospital Universitari General de Castelló — provides students with access to a modern tertiary hospital environment. As UJI's Medicine programme matures, its clinical training capacity and methodological refinement are expected to develop further.

## Admission Model

Admission to Medicine at UJI is governed by the **accesuniversitats** system of the Comunitat Valenciana, administered by the **Comissió Gestora de Processos Universitaris (CGPAU)**. Candidates apply through the regional portal and rank their preferred degree-university combinations. There is no direct application to UJI.

**The nota de admisión formula:**

```
Nota de admisión = (0.6 × Nota media Bachillerato) + (0.4 × EVAU general phase grade)
                 + (best 2 specific-phase subjects × ponderation coefficient)
```

- Base score maximum: 10.0000 points
- Specific-phase bonus maximum: 4.0000 additional points
- Absolute maximum nota de admisión: 14.0000

PCE results replace the EVAU components of the nota de admisión formula. Alternatively, EU secondary school graduates may present a Credencial UE for direct equivalence assessment.

## Admission Process

### For Spanish EVAU (PAU) Candidates

1. Complete Bachillerato and register for the EVAU in your region.
2. Sit both the general phase and, strategically, the specific-phase subjects with the highest ponderation coefficients for Medicine at UJI (ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED).
3. Calculate your estimated nota de admisión using the CGPAU formula.
4. During the accesuniversitats application window (ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED), register on the Comunitat Valenciana portal and rank university-degree combinations.
5. Await provisional allocation and confirm your place within the stated deadline.
6. Complete enrolment at UJI's Facultat de Ciències de la Salut.

### For International Students (PCE/UNED Pathway)

1. Verify your secondary school qualification is eligible for PCE/UNED access.
2. Register with UNED and sit the PCE examinations (typically May/June — ANNUAL_UPDATE_REQUIRED).
3. Request your Credencial UNED, which converts PCE results into a nota de admisión equivalent.
4. Participate in the accesuniversitats process using your Credencial UNED.
5. If accepted, obtain a student visa (non-EU applicants) and complete all enrolment documentation at UJI.

Non-EU international students must also verify general Spanish university access requirements with the Spanish Ministry of Education prior to applying.

Graduates of the Grado en Medicina at UJI are eligible to sit the **MIR (Médico Interno Residente)** national examination, the compulsory pathway to specialisation in Spain's public health system. The MIR is sat at the national level each year and is independent of the graduate's university of origin.
